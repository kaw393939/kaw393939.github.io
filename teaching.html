Teaching AI ​

I teach web development, web applications, and content management at NJIT, and I direct the undergraduate Enterprise AI program. Across those settings, I design assignments around a handoff. A classmate tries to buy a T-shirt. A reviewer asks what an automated check actually checked. Watching the person who built the work respond tells me something a finished screen cannot.

If you teach a course or mentor a new engineer, pick one decision you want to watch the learner make. In the Studios below, it might be choosing a buyer, calling the winner of a game, or finding out why a website is unreachable. If you’re here for your IS373 project, open the student guide to choose your next step.

The Question I Want Students to Ask Without Me ​

A rider requests Friday. Has the shop promised a repair? In my web applications Studio, every team can interview the fictional owner and ask a real classmate to try its answer. The same short project record gives students a story for an interview and gives me evidence for individual feedback and the next class. Read the bike-shop Studio story

Build something you can explain ​

I call these activities Studios. Students try a starter, choose one change, build it with help, and check what actually happened. A plain-text file called AGENTS.md tells the AI assistant when to help, what to ask, and when to wait. Open a starter below to see what that looks like in practice.

StudioA decision the student makes
Brand Studio · Intro to Website DevelopmentChoose the audience for a plain white T-shirt. Point to one design or wording choice that fits that buyer.
Rock, Paper, Scissors · Building Web ApplicationsPredict the outcome of a round from the player’s point of view. Check a test, then play it yourself.
Server Studio · Content Management SystemsPredict how a request will reach a calculator running on your server. Open the public address and see what happens.

In the Knowledge Lab, I use a club handbook to give students two different decisions. Can they find who organizes events? What should the app say about a meeting room the handbook never names? Use those two questions to check a RAG app.

Try a Studio in your course ​

Pick an assignment you already teach and a decision you want students to make. Rehearse the assignment once, playing a student who is stuck. Does the coach give a helpful hint, or does it make the decision for you?

The download includes a worked example, a student handout, and the coach’s instructions. The teacher copy contains answers. Share only LESSON.md and AGENTS.md with students. Download the teacher Studio starter · Read the research behind the starter

Learning beyond one assignment ​

The follow-up I care about is what happens on a fresh task, after the coach stops prompting. I describe the comparison I’d like to study in my research agenda. The AI career field guide connects classroom practice to responsibilities employers assign to engineers.

If you want to try a Studio in another course, send me the task and the decision you want the learner to own. I’d like to hear what they choose when you hand them the keyboard. Contact me through NJIT
