Teaching AI ​

After more than two decades at NJIT, I still look forward to handing a student’s work to someone who has never seen it. A classmate trying to buy a T-shirt can find a problem that everyone who built the store missed. The student gets feedback they can use straight away. As their teacher, I get to see how they decide what to change.

The Tests Passed. Who Won the Game? ​

My web applications starter passed twelve automated checks and still couldn’t settle a round of rock-paper-scissors. That was deliberate. The part I wanted the student to explain was what happens when the computer picks paper and the player picks rock. The feature follows that question through three courses. Read the classroom story

Build something you can explain ​

I call these activities Studios. Students try a starter, choose one change, build it with help, and check what actually happened. A plain-text file called AGENTS.md tells the AI assistant when to help, what to ask, and when to wait.

StudioA decision the student makes
Brand Studio · Intro to Website DevelopmentChoose the audience for a plain white T-shirt. Point to one design or wording choice that fits that buyer.
Rock, Paper, Scissors · Building Web ApplicationsPredict the outcome of a round from the player’s point of view. Check a test, then play it yourself.
Server Studio · Content Management SystemsPredict how a request will reach a calculator running on your server. Open the public address and see what happens.

For a project that works with documents, try the Knowledge Lab. Give the system a question it can answer and ask for the passage that supports its answer. Then ask for a detail the collection leaves out.

Try a Studio in your course ​

Pick an assignment you already teach and a decision you want students to make. Rehearse the assignment once, playing a student who is stuck. Does the coach give a helpful hint, or does it make the decision for you?

The download includes a worked example, a student handout, and the coach’s instructions. The teacher copy contains answers. Share only LESSON.md and AGENTS.md with students. Download the teacher Studio starter · Read the research behind the starter

Learning beyond one assignment ​

I’m investigating which decisions students can make when the next task looks different. The research agenda explains the question. Students choosing a portfolio project can use the AI career field guide to find a responsibility they want to practice.
