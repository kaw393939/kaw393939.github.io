What 50 AI Job Postings Ask Engineers to Do ​

All ten forward deployed roles in this sample mentioned working with customers. Six mentioned language models. What would you put in a portfolio to show you can do the job?

Keith Williams

Share on LinkedIn

Pick up two ads for an “AI engineer” and underline the verbs. One employer wants someone to sit with a customer, find out why a workflow stalls, and deliver a fix. Another wants someone to design tests that catch model failures before release. Those are two different weeks at work. They also suggest two different projects a student could build.

I read employer descriptions across five role families to help students decide what to practice. The 50-posting sample covers professional roles. Three separate internship examples show where a student might start. These examples describe the work employers requested. Visit the employer’s careers page to check availability, pay and eligibility before applying.

From a posting to a portfolio project ​

Google’s internship example below combines customer discovery with building a tool. Here’s one way to practice both. Ask a classmate to use a club handbook to find out who organizes events. Watch where they look. Build a search that returns the relevant passage, then hand the keyboard back. Keep the question, the passage they found, and the change their feedback led you to make. Those three pieces give an interviewer something specific to ask you about. Try the handbook search with five sample documents.

The short version ​

  • If you like working with people, look for customer discovery, workshops, and implementation. For a portfolio example, give a classmate the keyboard, observe one difficulty, fix it, and invite them to retest. See a classroom demo
  • If you like investigating failures, look for evaluation, observability, and testing. Show a test that caught a plausible wrong answer and explain how you knew it was wrong.
  • If you’re applying as a student, check graduation windows, work location, travel, and deadlines before you spend hours on an application.

Roles to understand ​

These are experienced professional roles. Read the job duties alongside the years of experience each employer requested. Posted ranges are not market averages. Compare base pay with base pay and note separately where equity, commission, or annualized figures enter the calculation.

Forward Deployed Software Engineer ​

The work: understand a customer's problem, scope a solution and deliver software that works in their environment. AI coding does not remove the need to integrate, test and own the result.

Example: OpenAI, NYC. $153,000–$325,000 base plus equity; 7+ years of professional full-stack experience, excluding internships. Relational databases, API systems and customer delivery; hybrid with three office days and up to 50% travel.

Read the official OpenAI FDE posting

Applied AI Engineer ​

The work: turn model capabilities into useful applications, often alongside enterprise customers. Build agents, test quality and explain architecture choices.

Example: Anthropic, Enterprise, NYC/San Francisco. Listed annual salary $200,000–$320,000; 4+ years of experience. Python/TypeScript, production LLM applications, agents, MCP and evaluations. Customer workshops are part of the role; three office days per week.

Read the official Anthropic posting

Applied AI Architect ​

The work: connect business needs to a viable technical design, including security, APIs, data and evaluation. This is more than selecting a model.

Example: OpenAI, NYC. Posted range $156,600–$276,000, with equity and commission also mentioned. The page notes multiple ranges, so confirm which compensation structure applies. This is an experienced customer-technical-strategy role involving cloud systems, RAG and evaluations.

Read the official Applied AI Architect posting

AI Deployment Manager — Builder ​

The work: help people adopt AI through practical workshops, useful workflows and technical enablement. Teaching and instructional design can be relevant alongside coding.

Example: OpenAI, NYC/San Francisco. $240,000 plus equity; 4+ years of relevant customer-facing or instructional experience. AI APIs, agents, evaluations and workshop design; three office days per week.

Read the official AI Deployment Manager posting

AI Systems Engineer — Codex Agents ​

The work: build the infrastructure around agents: execution environments, sandboxing, evaluation, logging, reliability, latency and cost control.

Example: OpenAI, San Francisco/NYC/Seattle. The posting emphasizes Rust/Python and production engineering. Pay is not reproduced here because it was not captured in this review.

How to read the counts ​

This is a purposive convenience sample. I selected distinct employer requisitions and excluded duplicate syndications and location variants. The forward deployed, agent, and evaluation groups each cover ten employers. Applied AI covers nine and architecture eight. HiddenLayer contributed three distinct architecture functions, giving security work a larger place in that group.

The evidence included 38 employer descriptions with an application route. Eleven more came from indexed employer material, with application status unconfirmed. One came from an official university PDF. The DoorDash application page was readable, but the vacancy could not be confirmed. Four other links stopped serving a usable posting and were excluded.

Counts include duties, preferred skills, and allowed alternatives. Eight mentions of a language in ten postings do not mean eight employers required it. Each chart counts the skills relevant to that role family. The records underneath show what each employer actually said and how the source was checked.

Turn the evidence into a learning plan ​

One small project can demonstrate different responsibilities. For a forward deployed role, show how a user’s feedback changed the work. For applied AI, compare a feature against a baseline and a difficult test case. An architect could use the same project to explain the data flow, access controls, operating costs, and a build-or-buy decision. An evaluation engineer could track disagreement between two reviewers. An agent engineer could trace a failed tool call and show what happens next.

These are examples for practice, not hiring guarantees. Check enrollment, work authorization, location, travel, and clearance requirements in the employer’s posting.

What should you learn? ​

  1. Build something small. Connect a Python or TypeScript application to an API or database. Use Git, write a test, and deploy it somewhere a second person can try.
  2. Trace an answer. Connect a model to a source, then ask for a detail that source leaves out. Open the cited passage and decide which part of the answer it supports. Test your app with five kinds of questions.
  3. Measure a result. Run a small test set, record one failure, and check the effect of a change. If cost, latency, privacy, or security matters to the user, say how you checked it.
  4. Hand over the keyboard. Ask someone who never helped build the system to try it. Let them tell you where an instruction, screen, or result surprised them.
  5. Make a claim you can defend. If your change saved time, show the before and after measurements. If you tried it with fictional data, say so.

Credentials. Read the individual posting. The internship examples specify enrollment and graduation windows. A certification may help when an employer asks for it. The required degree and experience vary by role.

Turn research into a plan ​

Choose two employer descriptions and put them side by side. Keep the original links, eligibility, location and pay basis. Check application requirements with the employer. Underline one responsibility they share. Then choose one gap you can address with a project small enough to finish.

If you publish the work as a portfolio or research site, give a reader three things: the employer requirement, an example of you doing that work, and enough evidence for someone to check the result. Ask a peer for one specific improvement, make the change, and link the revision. Keep private application details out of the public repository.

How this guide is maintained ​

The dataset and employer links let readers inspect the basis for each finding. Postings can close, move or change. For an application, consult the employer directly. For a portfolio project, use the recorded responsibilities to choose a skill and demonstrate it.
