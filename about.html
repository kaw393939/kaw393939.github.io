About Keith Williams ​

I’m Keith Williams, a Senior University Lecturer in Informatics at the New Jersey Institute of Technology and director of its B.S. in Enterprise AI. I’ve built software for learning and collaboration, taught mobile development at ZCAS University in Zambia, and worked with Zambia’s Ministry of Finance on digital public services.

Across those settings, I keep coming back to a practical question. What happens when the person who built the system hands it to the person who needs it? It shapes how I design courses, test software, and think about human judgment in AI. Inside my classroom · My engineering case studies

Where it started ​

My first home computer was a Tandy TRS-80 Color Computer 2. My father worked as a mainframe systems lead operator at Alcoa. Now I had a machine I could program myself, and I started teaching myself how.

Later, through AmeriCorps, I taught children about computers at an after-school center in Pittsburgh. I’ve also worked with high schools. I still think about the person sitting down at a computer for the first time, wondering what they’re allowed to try.

Building things, teaching people ​

I started teaching at NJIT as an adjunct. I’ve developed courses and helped shape the Web and Information Systems program. Along the way I built Showedme.net, a web application framework and learning management system, and Wrkbench, an iOS collaboration app.

Today my students use AI coding assistants. In the classroom exercises I call Studios, I can give the assistant a job and reserve a decision for the student. A learner can ask for help, test a prediction, and see what they would change. Try a Studio

Learning beyond my own classroom ​

A conversation with an NJIT graduate student from Zambia led to a progressive web application workshop at ZCAS University for students and staff. In Zambia I also worked with the Ministry of Finance on an initiative to digitize public services. An online form only helps if the person completing it can actually finish the government transaction. That question about delivery has stayed with me. NJIT on teaching in Zambia · NJIT on digitizing government services

What I’m working on now ​

Two questions keep coming up in my work with AI. How do we tell what someone learned when AI helped them build? How do we trace a confident answer back to the evidence behind it? My research agenda describes the studies I’d like to run. The Knowledge Lab gives readers a retrieval exercise to try, and my project portfolio links to the software and teaching materials behind the work.

In MetaVis, my Swift and Metal video-editing prototype, checking a rendered frame and checking an exported video tell you different things. I wrote up the tests so another engineer can inspect the distinction. Inside MetaVis

Life Tracker explains how I carry the same habit into everyday work with an AI assistant. A separate proposal, EverydayAI Newark, imagines paid workshops helping fund free community learning. It is a proposal, not an operating program.
