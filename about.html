About Keith Williams ​

I’m a Senior University Lecturer at the New Jersey Institute of Technology and director of its B.S. in Enterprise AI. When a student shows me a working project, I like to hand it to someone else. What happens when that person tries something the student never anticipated? That’s often where the most interesting part of the lesson begins. Inside my classroom

Where it started ​

My first home computer was a Tandy TRS-80 Color Computer 2. My father worked as a mainframe systems lead operator at Alcoa. Now I had a machine I could program myself, and I started teaching myself how.

Later, through AmeriCorps, I taught children about computers at an after-school center in Pittsburgh. I’ve also worked with high schools. I still think about the person sitting down at a computer for the first time, wondering what they’re allowed to try.

Building things, teaching people ​

I started teaching at NJIT as an adjunct. I’ve developed courses and helped shape the Web and Information Systems program. Along the way I built Showedme.net, a web application framework and learning management system, and Wrkbench, an iOS collaboration app.

Today my students use AI coding assistants. In the classroom exercises I call Studios, I can give the assistant a job and reserve a decision for the student. A learner can ask for help, test a prediction, and see what they would change. Try a Studio

Learning beyond my own classroom ​

In Zambia, I taught software engineering and worked with government teams on digital public services, user experience, and prototypes. A prototype gave us something concrete to put in front of the person trying to use a service. We could watch where they got stuck and ask what they had expected to happen. NJIT on teaching in Zambia · NJIT on digitizing government services

What I’m working on now ​

Two questions keep coming up in my work with AI. How do we tell what someone learned when AI helped them build? How do we trace a confident answer back to the evidence behind it? My research agenda describes the studies I’d like to run. The Knowledge Lab gives readers a retrieval exercise to try, and my project portfolio links to the software and teaching materials behind the work.

I also write about ordinary uses of AI. Life Tracker is the method I use to follow a task from a conversation to a verified result. A separate proposal, EverydayAI Newark, imagines paid workshops helping fund free community learning and opportunities for students to show their work. It is a proposal, not an operating program.
