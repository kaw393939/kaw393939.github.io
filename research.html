Rethinking Education with AI ​

In my NJIT Studios, I can change one rule in a game and ask the person who built it which test should fail. In MetaVis, my Swift and Metal editing prototype, a reference frame can pass a pixel check while the exported video still needs a sound check. Both experiences led me to ask what a test actually lets another person conclude.

I’m pursuing that question in two directions. In education, I want to study what someone can do after AI assistance ends. In knowledge systems, I want to find out how a human reviewer can trace a claim to its evidence and spot where the system needs attention. These are proposed studies and engineering questions. The technical case studies document the work completed so far.

Can you use what you learned somewhere new? ​

In a classroom Studio, the AI coach can offer hints and help run checks. A follow-up problem gives the student a fresh choice. Which test would they run first? What evidence would make them change their mind?

I’d like to work with another instructor on a common follow-up problem after students have had different amounts of coaching. We could compare three observations from each attempt. Who suggested the first check? What did the learner predict? How did they explain the result? Read the classroom feature · Explore the research behind the Studio

What should count as evidence of learning? ​

A student changes their mind halfway through a project. The final version may look much like a classmate’s, while the change itself tells a different story. I want to know whether another instructor can identify that decision from a selected exchange, an annotated draft, or a brief demonstration. I also want to know how much teaching time each approach takes.

Building knowledge a reader can check ​

Two records describe the same event and give different dates. A search system can retrieve both. A reader needs to see the passage behind each date and understand why the discrepancy remains.

The systems series follows the problem from source documents through retrieval, software checks, and human review. Start with MetaVis to see how I evaluated a working prototype. The preliminary doctoral proposal and reading map lay out what I still want to test. Browse the systems series · Read the MetaVis case study · Open the proposal · Explore the reading map

How do we work with AI responsibly? ​

An assistant that can use tools can turn a misunderstanding into a real change. I want to know which actions a person should review, what evidence the assistant should preserve, and how to correct a mistake. Life Tracker describes how I record and check actions in a small personal workflow. Ordo documents a broader system for human-directed AI work.
