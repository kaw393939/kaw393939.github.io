# AI Careers

**What are employers actually asking for, and what can you start learning now?**

AI is changing the work people do. The useful question is not just “Will there be jobs?” It is “What problems are organizations hiring people to solve?” This field guide connects real employer postings to skills you can practice and projects you can explain.

**Sources checked: November 12, 2026.** This is a selected research snapshot, not a comprehensive job board. Openings, pay and eligibility can change. Check the employer's page before applying.

[Explore roles](#roles-to-understand) · [Find internships](#internships-start-here) · [Plan your skills](#what-should-you-learn) · [Build your research site](#turn-research-into-a-plan)

## The short version

- **Production is only part of the job.** Employers also ask for discovery, communication, evaluation, security, integration and measurable business results.
- **FDE is a useful starting point, not the only path.** Forward deployed engineering predates the current AI boom. Look at the actual work, not whether a title sounds new.
- **Start before graduation.** Internships have specific eligibility windows. A strong project helps you demonstrate skills, but neither AI fluency nor a portfolio guarantees a job.

## Roles to understand

These examples are **experienced professional roles**, not new-graduate salary promises. They show directions to work toward. Posted ranges are not market averages; salary, hourly pay, equity and commission are different things.

### Forward Deployed Software Engineer

**The work:** understand a customer's problem, scope a solution and deliver software that works in their environment. AI coding does not remove the need to integrate, test and own the result.

**Example:** OpenAI, NYC. **$153,000–$325,000 base plus equity**; 7+ years of professional full-stack experience, excluding internships. Relational databases, API systems and customer delivery; hybrid with three office days and up to 50% travel.

[Read the official OpenAI FDE posting](https://openai.com/careers/forward-deployed-software-engineer-nyc-new-york-city/)

### Applied AI Engineer

**The work:** turn model capabilities into useful applications, often alongside enterprise customers. Build agents, test quality and explain architecture choices.

**Example:** Anthropic, Enterprise, NYC/San Francisco. Listed annual salary **$200,000–$320,000**; 4+ years of experience. Python/TypeScript, production LLM applications, agents, MCP and evaluations. Customer workshops are part of the role; three office days per week.

[Read the official Anthropic posting](https://job-boards.greenhouse.io/anthropic/jobs/5057647008)

### Applied AI Architect

**The work:** connect business needs to a viable technical design, including security, APIs, data and evaluation. This is more than selecting a model.

**Example:** OpenAI, NYC. Posted range **$156,600–$276,000**, with equity and commission also mentioned. The page notes multiple ranges, so confirm which compensation structure applies. This is an experienced customer-technical-strategy role involving cloud systems, RAG and evaluations.

[Read the official Applied AI Architect posting](https://openai.com/careers/applied-ai-architect-new-york-city/)

### AI Deployment Manager — Builder

**The work:** help people adopt AI through practical workshops, useful workflows and technical enablement. Teaching and instructional design can be relevant alongside coding.

**Example:** OpenAI, NYC/San Francisco. **$240,000 plus equity**; 4+ years of relevant customer-facing or instructional experience. AI APIs, agents, evaluations and workshop design; three office days per week.

[Read the official AI Deployment Manager posting](https://openai.com/careers/ai-deployment-manager-builder-new-york-city/)

### AI Systems Engineer — Codex Agents

**The work:** build the infrastructure around agents: execution environments, sandboxing, evaluation, logging, reliability, latency and cost control.

**Example:** OpenAI, San Francisco/NYC/Seattle. The posting emphasizes Rust/Python and production engineering. Pay is not reproduced here because it was not captured in this review.

[Read the official AI Systems Engineer posting](https://openai.com/careers/ai-systems-engineer-codex-agents-san-francisco/)

## What should you learn?

My interpretation of these postings is straightforward: **learn to build, explain, evaluate and improve a system that solves a real problem.** Programming fundamentals still matter. The examples do not establish that conventional software jobs have disappeared.

1. **Build:** practice Python or TypeScript, APIs, SQL, Git, tests and deployment. Use AI assistance, but be able to debug what it produces.
2. **Ground:** connect a model to relevant data. Compare retrieval strategies, preserve sources and handle missing information. The <a href="/knowledge-lab/">Knowledge Lab</a> is a place to start.
3. **Evaluate:** create a small test set, measure quality and failure cases, and log latency and token cost. Include privacy and security checks.
4. **Communicate:** interview a potential user, write requirements and explain tradeoffs without jargon. Demonstrate the system to someone who did not build it.
5. **Show value:** compare the old workflow with the new one. Report what you actually measured, not invented time savings or simulated results presented as real outcomes.

**Degree or certification?** Follow the individual posting. The internship examples specify enrollment and graduation requirements. A certificate may structure learning, but it is not a substitute for the requested experience, and this sample does not establish a universal certification requirement.

## Turn research into a plan

Build a small research website before committing to a large project. The point is to organize information so **you** can understand it and make a decision, not to generate more pages.

- Separate **roles, internships, skills and next steps**. Record employer, location, eligibility, pay basis, deadline, source URL and date checked.
- Choose two roles and compare their requirements. Find the overlap and one meaningful gap in your current skills.
- Build a small proof of that skill: a useful application, a test suite, a cost comparison or a clear technical walkthrough.
- Ask a peer to review the research and open an issue with one concrete improvement. Make the change, verify it and close the issue with evidence.
- Keep the presentation concise. Use a chart, diagram or optional audio summary only when it helps you understand the material. Do not upload private application details to a public repository.
