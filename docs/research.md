# Rethinking Education with AI

A student and an AI assistant build a working program. The next day I change one rule. Can the student predict which part will break? In a different setting, a reader asks a question and gets an answer with two citations. Can they see when those sources disagree?

Both questions start with something that looks finished. I want to study what it takes for a person to inspect it and decide what to do next. These are proposed studies and engineering questions. The technical case studies document the work completed so far.

## Can you use what you learned somewhere new?

In a classroom Studio, the AI coach can offer hints and help run checks. A follow-up problem gives the student a fresh choice. Which test would they run first? What evidence would make them change their mind?

I’d like to compare students’ explanations, debugging choices, and solutions to unfamiliar problems after different amounts of coaching. To learn anything from the comparison, instructors need similar follow-up tasks and a consistent way to interpret the work. ## What should count as evidence of learning?

A student changes their mind halfway through a project. The final version may look much like a classmate’s, while the change itself tells a different story. I want to know whether another instructor can identify that decision from a selected exchange, an annotated draft, or a brief demonstration. I also want to know how much teaching time each approach takes.

## Building knowledge a reader can check

![A magnifying glass enlarges a marked spot on layers of blue tracing paper.](/images/editorial/evidence-desk.jpg)

Two records describe the same event and give different dates. A search system can retrieve both. A reader needs to see the passage behind each date and understand why the discrepancy remains.

## How do we work with AI responsibly?

An assistant that can use tools can turn a misunderstanding into a real change. I want to know which actions a person should review, what evidence the assistant should preserve, and how to correct a mistake. [Life Tracker](/life-tracker) describes how I record and check actions in a small personal workflow. [Ordo](https://github.com/kaw393939/ordo) documents a broader system for human-directed AI work.
