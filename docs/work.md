# Selected work and professional record

**I've spent more than two decades teaching software engineering, designing technical learning experiences, and building software in industry and education.** My current work investigates how AI can make information more useful without making evidence or human judgment disappear.

This is a selected record—not an exhaustive CV. I distinguish independently documented work, public implementations, historical projects described in my own account, and prospective research.

## University teaching and curriculum

I began teaching at NJIT in 2002 and am now a **Senior University Lecturer in Informatics**, working on the **B.S. in Enterprise AI**. My teaching spans introductory web development, advanced web applications, mobile applications, APIs, open-source development, and systems work.

My [NJIT faculty profile](https://people.njit.edu/profile/kwilliam) lists the courses I've taught. [NJIT's undergraduate programs](https://computing.njit.edu/undergraduate-degrees) describe the Enterprise AI degree. [NJIT's published syllabi](https://digitalcommons.njit.edu/info-syllabi/) provide dated examples of my teaching assignments.

I helped develop the Web Systems curriculum and am now involved in its evolution toward Enterprise AI. My earlier [personal portfolio account](https://eaikw.com/about/) describes founding the Web Systems program, creating eight regularly offered courses, and teaching more than 10,000 students. **Those counts are my own historical account, not independently audited totals**; the institutional sources substantiate the long teaching record and the range of courses, but do not establish those exact figures.

### Teaching AI before it became a standard course topic

In [May 2023, NJIT News](https://news.njit.edu/njit-faculty-artificial-intelligence-learning-opportunity) documented how I was using generative AI to construct simulated client-work experiences. My students would extract requirements from interviews, document software, and define acceptance criteria. The core question was already about what people should understand, test, and decide as AI automates more programming work.

The [IS117 Brand Studio](https://github.com/kaw393939/is117-brand-studio) is a current public teaching template for a spec-guided, AI-assisted branding assignment. Its shopping bag is a demo rather than a real payment system. The [Ordo Knowledge Lab](https://keithwilliams.org/knowledge-lab/) is a published collection of student-oriented materials on retrieval, data systems, models, and evidence.

## International education and public-sector technology

In **2019**, I taught progressive web applications at Zambia's **ZCAS University**. Participants included students and university staff; NJIT reported that they began work on a student-registration application. [NJIT News, August 2019](https://news.njit.edu/inspiring-students-njit-africa).

In **2020**, NJIT reported on my work with **Zambia's Ministry of Finance**, supporting e-government service development and bringing Agile practices into its software process. The report describes an initiative in its early stages, not a completed national rollout. [NJIT News, June 2020](https://news.njit.edu/njit-lecturer-helps-digitize-government-services-zambia).

These experiences reinforced an enduring lesson: an information system succeeds only when the surrounding people, institutions, and processes can use it.

## A continuing question

Across media engineering, learning systems, fictional knowledge universes, and historical archives, I kept encountering the same problem: AI can read, write, and propose decisions faster than we can personally verify them. It can also do useful work with incomplete information, but only when the assumptions, evidence and consequences are understood.

My current research asks how to make that verification **source-grounded, proportionate to the task, and economical**—then turn the resulting knowledge into personalized educational and decision-support experiences.
