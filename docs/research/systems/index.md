# Evidence-Governed AI Systems

**How can we turn unstructured information into useful, personalized knowledge artifacts while preserving evidence, authority, and the scarce attention of human experts?**

This is my developing research program. It grew out of building software and exploring the limits of AI-assisted development. The articles distinguish *implemented systems*, *documented design proposals*, and *hypotheses not yet tested in controlled studies*. They are not peer-reviewed publications.

## The practitioner behind the research

I am **director of NJIT's B.S. in Enterprise AI**, having spent 23 years teaching and developing technical curricula. I created eight IS courses, created the Web and Information Systems degree, and co-developed BSEAI with colleagues including Brook Wu. My engineering history includes published software, international teaching, digital public-services work and native Swift/Metal research experiments.

## A connected research vision

**[From claims to navigable knowledge](./vision-and-interface)** connects the full experience: evidence-backed claims as the canonical knowledge model; distinct page-navigation and evidentiary graphs; Wikipedia/Wikidata as attributed discovery sources; semantic search; dynamically rendered dossiers; and a decision-oriented intelligence briefing with original-citation drill-down.

**[Why UAP records are an unusually demanding testbed](./uap-research-testbed)** explains exactly what the case can and cannot demonstrate, what source acquisition remains to be done, and why a second independently labeled benchmark is essential.

## The research in one view

- **Knowledge:** acquire multimodal material, construct claims and relationships, and retain access to exact sources, contradictory evidence, and uncertainty.
- **Orchestration:** use self-describing sensors, deterministic tools, local/frontier models, and authorized reviewers to process work appropriately.
- **Governance:** distinguish observations from qualified knowledge; record what was approved, by whom, under which policy and evidence.
- **Economics:** spend expensive human judgment on decisions where it changes the outcome; measure errors, recovery, compute, and review time.
- **Outcomes:** generate reliable personalized educational, business, and media artifacts from qualified knowledge.

## Questions connecting the work

**AI is increasingly able to read, write and recommend at enormous scale.** The hard question is not whether every piece of information can be made perfectly true, but what level and form of justification it needs **for a particular use**. [My epistemology and decision-theory research position](./epistemic-governance) separates source fidelity, process reliability, claim support, fitness for purpose and authority.
