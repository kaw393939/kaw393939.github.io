03 / A WAY OF WORKING

Stay curious.
Make something useful.

I work with AI by giving it context, trying things, checking the result, and improving the next attempt. The conversation matters, but so does what actually works.

Follow the work on GitHub
  1. 01

    Ask a better question.

    Start with the people, the problem, and the context. Give curiosity a useful direction.

  2. 02

    Make the intent clear.

    Write down the goal, the constraints, and what a good result should look like.

  3. 03

    Build. Test. Learn.

    Turn ideas into something tangible. Evaluate the result, examine the gaps, and improve it.

  4. 04

    Keep judgment human.

    Use AI to extend capability while staying accountable for the decisions that matter.

04 / QUESTIONS WORTH TESTING

What should learning
look like now?

If a tool can write the code or draft the answer, how do we help someone become a better thinker? I don't think the answer is simply to pretend the tool isn't there.

Learning beyond the generated answer

How can AI-assisted assignments develop independent problem-solving, transfer of learning, and the ability to evaluate generated work?

02 / OPEN QUESTION

Assessment when AI can produce the artifact

What evidence should count as learning: process, explanation, testing, judgment, collaboration, or the finished product?

03 / OPEN QUESTION

Working with AI without handing over judgment

How do we give AI useful work, check its evidence, and stay responsible for the decisions it helps us make?

These are questions guiding exploration and possible future studies, not claims of completed empirical research.

05 / A LITTLE BACKGROUND

An educator.
A builder.
Always a learner.

My father worked with mainframe computers at Alcoa. I grew up around that world, and a TRS-80 Color Computer 2 for Christmas in 1983 gave me a place to start making things of my own.

That curiosity has taken me from teaching children in an AmeriCorps program in Pittsburgh to classrooms at NJIT and software engineering work in Zambia. Along the way, I've built software, developed courses, and learned a great deal from the people I teach.

AI is the next chapter, not a new identity. I'm still interested in the same thing: helping people turn a confusing new technology into something they can understand and use.
