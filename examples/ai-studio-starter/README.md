# Make a Studio from an assignment you already teach

**Teacher copy.** This README includes the example answers. Share only **LESSON.md** and **AGENTS.md** with students.

A Studio gives students something to make, one decision to own, and a way to find out whether their decision holds up. The teacher decides when AI can help and when it should wait.

## Start here

1. In an ordinary AI chat, attach or paste **TEACHER-STUDIO.md** and the assignment you already use. Type **“Help me turn this into a Studio.”** A rough idea works too.
2. Choose one question you want students to learn to ask for themselves. The coach helps draft a short practice turn and a fresh case. Play a student and try “I'm stuck. Can you do it?” Say **“back to design”** to change the coach’s reply.
3. Take away **LESSON.md** for students and **AGENTS.md**, plain-text instructions for their coach. In a chat, copy the labeled outputs into files or paste them directly. Use your existing hand-in and rubric. If students are working without AI, give them the lesson and ask the coach’s questions aloud.

## Try the example in five minutes

Attach the included **LESSON.md** and **AGENTS.md** in a fresh chat and type **“Start my Studio.”** You advise a help-desk manager using ten fictional wait times. A pencil and a partner work too.

**Teacher notes.** Before the change, the mean wait is 10 minutes and the median is 5. Afterward, both are 7. Replacing the original 30 with 5 makes the earlier mean and median both 5. Listen for which measure the student chooses and which claim it supports.

In the fresh case, the manager cares about visitors who have ten minutes between classes. Let the learner decide what to check. Four of five waits before the change and five of five afterward were at most ten minutes. The records omit service time and travel time. A learner who asks whether these visitors can finish their errand has spotted something a correct calculation alone cannot settle. If a hint surfaced that question, keep the hint visible.

## Listen to the work

If communication is part of the goal, try this demo question: **“Show me one moment when something you said changed the work.”** The learner can choose a short exchange, include the hint or question that preceded it, and point to one edit or check. A marked-up draft or spoken demo can show the same decision. Keep this in the normal private hand-in. No chat export is needed.

For a code review, ask the next person to reproduce one result. For a writing assignment, ask them to inspect the passage supporting a disputed claim. Either can fit inside the feedback students already give.
